What is Gap Analysis?
Gap analysis compares existing solutions with what DriveSense offers, highlighting the unmet needs and shortcomings that justify your project's existence.

Existing Systems & Their Gaps
1. Traditional Dashcams (Nextbase, Vantrue, etc.)
These are purely passive recording devices. They capture video but have zero intelligence built in — no behavior detection, no scoring, no alerts. A human must manually review hours of footage to find any issue. They also require dedicated hardware installation, making them costly and inaccessible to everyday drivers.
Core gap: No intelligence, no feedback, no automation.

2. GPS Navigation Apps (Google Maps, Waze, Apple Maps)
These apps are excellent at routing and traffic but completely blind to how you drive. They may show a speed limit warning, but they never detect harsh braking, aggressive cornering, or fatigue. There is no driver profile, no history, and no safety scoring whatsoever.
Core gap: Navigation-only — zero behavior awareness.

3. Commercial Fleet Management Systems (Samsara, Geotab, Verizon Connect)
These systems are the closest to DriveSense in terms of features — they track vehicles, score drivers, and detect events. However, they are built exclusively for corporate fleet operators, not individuals. They require expensive proprietary hardware dongles (OBD-II devices, dashcam kits), cost hundreds of dollars annually per vehicle, and are completely closed-source. A student, new driver, or individual commuter cannot access them.
Core gap: Enterprise-only, expensive, hardware-dependent, inaccessible to individuals.

4. Insurance Telematics Apps (Progressive Snapshot, Allstate Drivewise)
These apps do collect driving data through your phone, but their purpose is purely to calculate your insurance premium — not to help you become a better driver. Feedback is delayed (weekly or monthly), vague, and non-actionable. The AI model behind the scoring is proprietary and hidden from the user.
Core gap: Profit-driven, delayed feedback, no real-time coaching.

5. Academic & Research ADAS Systems
Research papers on driver monitoring systems often achieve impressive results using LiDAR, radar, infrared cameras, and custom embedded hardware. These are lab-grade solutions that are technically powerful but practically unusable — they cost thousands of dollars in hardware, require calibration by engineers, and cannot run on a smartphone. They exist only in controlled test environments.
Core gap: Not deployable in real-world consumer settings.

The 6 Critical Gaps DriveSense Fills
Gap 1 — No accessible real-time behavior detection
All capable systems require dedicated hardware. DriveSense uses the accelerometer, gyroscope, and GPS already present in every smartphone — making it zero-hardware and universally accessible.
Gap 2 — No individual-level driver scoring
Driver scoring exists only in fleet and insurance contexts, both with hidden agendas. DriveSense gives every individual driver a transparent, explainable 0–100 safety score after every trip.
Gap 3 — Rule-based detection, not intelligent learning
Existing consumer apps use fixed thresholds (e.g., "speed > 80 = flag"). DriveSense applies ML models that learn patterns over time, reducing false positives and adapting to driving context like road type and time of day.
Gap 4 — Zero drowsiness detection in consumer apps
Fatigue is one of the top causes of road accidents, yet not a single consumer-facing app detects it. DriveSense introduces camera-based eye tracking or steering micro-correction pattern analysis to flag fatigue early.
Gap 5 — No actionable personalized feedback
Even systems that score drivers never explain what to change or how. DriveSense generates specific, context-aware recommendations — telling the driver exactly which behavior to fix and estimating the score improvement.
Gap 6 — Poor visualization and reporting
Raw numbers and basic logs don't help drivers understand their behavior. DriveSense presents rich visual dashboards with trend charts, heatmaps, behavior breakdowns, and trip-by-trip comparisons that are genuinely understandable.
